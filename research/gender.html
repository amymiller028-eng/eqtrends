EQ & Gender

Overall, women and men rate their emotional intelligence about the same. The difference is in the skills. Women tend to rate themselves higher in Social Awareness, reading other people and what’s really going on with them. Men tend to rate themselves higher in Self-Management, keeping their reactions in check.

We first saw this pattern in 2009, when Travis Bradberry and Jean Greaves found men had caught up with women in Self-Management, a shift they credited to changing social norms. It still holds today, across 689,586 assessments and nearly every region of the world.

What other research says

The bigger picture

A 2026 meta-analysis in Emotion combined 1,428 samples covering 609,278 people in 76 countries, drawing on published and unpublished research through 2023. Two findings stand out.

1

Women scored higher on overall emotional intelligence, on average. But how much higher depended on the skill. For some skills the gap was large, and for others it was small or gone.

2

Women’s advantage was strongest for skills involving other people’s emotions, and weaker for skills involving their own.

Why our overall scores still tie

How EQ is measured matters. In a self-assessment, like ours, people rate how often they show certain behaviors, such as staying calm under pressure or noticing how others feel. In an ability test, people have to show the skill, for example by identifying the emotion in a photo of a face, and their answers are scored right or wrong. The study found the gap between women and men depends a lot on which kind of test is used.

That lines up with our data, where the biggest difference is in Social Awareness, the skill most focused on other people. The gaps in the study also varied by leadership position, industry, region and the year the data was collected.

What it means for you

Your gender won’t tell you your EQ.

1

It’s a tie.

Overall, women and men rate their EQ almost exactly the same, in nearly every region we measured.

2

Different strengths.

Men tend to rate Self-Management higher, and women Social Awareness. A group average says little about any one person.

3

Start with your lowest skill.

The best place to grow is your own lowest skill, whatever group you’re in.

Source: 731,369 Emotional Intelligence Appraisal self-assessments, 2021–2026, from test-takers around the world. Gender comparisons use the 689,586 (94%) from people who identified as a woman or a man. Another 41,783 people chose another option or preferred not to answer.
