EQ & Leader Level

EQ climbs with almost every step up the ladder, until the very top. Vice presidents score highest, and CEOs score below every level from manager up.

We first reported that EQ drops off at the top in “Heartless Bosses?” in Harvard Business Review in 2005, and again in Emotional Intelligence 2.0 in 2009. Back then, scores peaked with managers and fell steeply from director up. Today the climb goes further, but the top still dips.

EQ by job level

Each point is the average overall EQ score for one job level, from employees on the left to CEOs on the right. Click a point to see that level’s four skills.

EQ rises with almost every step up. Employees average 73.6, supervisors 75.0 and managers 76.0. Directors, vice presidents and senior vice presidents score highest, at about 77. That’s a climb of more than three points from the first job to the senior ranks.

Then it bends. C-level executives come in a little lower, at 76.7, and CEOs drop to 75.4, below every level from manager up. The people at the very top of an organization score about the same as a typical supervisor.

Leaders score higher on every skill

EQ is made up of four skills. Each row is one skill. The gray dot is the average for employees, the green dot is the average for vice presidents, and the number is how much higher vice presidents score.

EmployeesVice presidents

Vice presidents score higher than employees on all four EQ skills. Self-Management rises the most, up 5.1 points from employee to vice president. Social Awareness rises the least, up 2.3 points.

In other words, the biggest difference between people at the start of their careers and senior leaders isn’t reading other people. It’s staying in control of your own reactions under pressure.

CEOs are the exception at the top. They don’t score higher than vice presidents on any of the four skills, but they still score higher than employees on all four.

Why does EQ dip at the top?

One likely reason is that the higher people go, the less day-to-day contact they tend to have with the people around them, and the fewer chances they get to practice. EQ isn’t something you achieve once and keep. It’s a skill that stays sharp through use. Some leaders are also promoted for what they know rather than how well they lead people.

At the same time, EQ is increasingly required to move up. A study of thousands of executive-search job descriptions over 17 years found growing demand for social skills like listening, empathizing and understanding others’ reactions, especially in larger, more complex organizations.

Job level usually goes hand in hand with age. The higher up people are in an organization, the older they tend to be. So could age be the reason EQ seems to rise with leader level?

Could age be the reason?

One way to check is to compare generations within the same job level. If age were driving the climb, older generations would still score higher than younger ones in the same role. Each row is a job level, and each dot is a generation’s average overall EQ. Pick a generation to highlight it.

When we compare people at the same level, the generations land much closer together. Among employees, all four generations average between 73.4 and 73.8. Among managers, Gen Z (76.5) and Baby Boomers (76.6) are nearly tied.

So age isn’t the main story. People in the same role score about the same, whatever their generation, which points to the role itself.

Why a leader’s EQ matters

A leader’s mood and behavior reach more people, and carry more weight, than most. Research backs that up. A 2025 review of 101 studies linked leaders’ EQ with better leadership behaviors and performance outcomes, including how effective leaders are, which suggests EQ matters well beyond getting along with people. A 2018 meta-analysis found that leaders’ EQ is linked to how well their employees perform, in every culture studied. When we looked at executives in 2009, those with the highest EQ were also the best performers.

A title doesn’t automatically tell you someone’s EQ. The higher you go, the easier it is to lose touch and the harder it is to find time to practice. The good news is that EQ can be built. In our data, people who retest score an average of 5 points higher.
